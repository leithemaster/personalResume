I design it.Then I build it.

I'm Liem Nguyen, a UX designer who also writes production code. I've worked on both sides of the handoff, and most of what goes wrong in software goes wrong right there.

At Geocent I led the design of an internal support portal for Marines, from user flows to stakeholder approval, and handed it to developers I'd been working beside the whole time. At Sev1Tech I was the developer: building features for NexGen at USCIS in React and Spring Boot, and shaping their design as I went. Before that I worked at Fund Diet. [one line on Fund Diet]

Doing both changes how I do each. My designs come with their cost already counted, and my code keeps the intent of the design it came from.

How I think about design

  1. Load-bearing decisions first.

    Before color or type, settle what the thing has to hold: the flows, the states, the data behind them. Polish on a weak structure is wasted work.

  2. Access is structural.

    A ramp poured with the foundation costs almost nothing. Bolted on later, it's expensive and it shows. Section 508 and WCAG work the same way.

  3. The drawing isn't the building.

    A mockup is a promise. I stay with the work through handoff and build, because the finished form is where a design is proven.
